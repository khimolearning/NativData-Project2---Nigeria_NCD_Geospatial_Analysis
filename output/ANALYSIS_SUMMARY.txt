NativData Project 2: Nigeria NCD Geospatial Analysis
ANALYSIS SUMMARY
================================================================================

PROJECT SCOPE
=============

This project maps type 2 diabetes and cancer across Nigeria using real,
published epidemiological data. It answers two questions:

1. Where is diabetes burden highest in Nigeria? (Answer: clear South-North divide)
2. Where do we KNOW cancer burden in Nigeria? (Answer: only 6 registries exist)

The second question reveals a critical infrastructure gap.

================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

FINDING 1: DIABETES HAS A SOUTH-NORTH GRADIENT
----------------------------------------------

Zone                 Prevalence      95% CI Range
South South          9.8%            7.2-12.4%
North East           5.9%            2.4-9.4%
South West           5.5%            4.0-7.1%
South East           4.6%            3.4-5.9%
North Central        3.8%            2.9-4.7%
North West           3.0%            1.7-4.3%

INTERPRETATION:
- South South's prevalence is 3.3x higher than North West
- This is statistically significant and consistent across studies
- Pattern mirrors Project 1's Pidgin/finance findings: South-North divide
- Likely drivers: urbanization (South is more urban), income (oil wealth in
  South South), lifestyle factors (Western diet adoption in urban areas)

EVIDENCE:
- Data source: Uloko et al. 2018 meta-analysis (peer-reviewed)
- Sample: Pooled data from 30+ studies across Nigeria 2005-2018
- Quality: Published in a Springer journal, proper 95% confidence intervals

================================================================================

FINDING 2: CANCER SURVEILLANCE IS CONCENTRATED IN THE SOUTH
-----------------------------------------------------------

Registry Location    State              Zone          ASR per 100,000
Ibadan              Oyo                South West     98.5
Abuja               Federal Capital    North Central  98.5
Ekiti               Ekiti              South West     78.9
Enugu               Enugu              South East     78.4
Calabar             Cross River        South South    58.3

REGISTRIES BY ZONE:
- South West: 2 registries
- South East: 2 registries
- South South: 1 registry
- North Central: 1 registry (Abuja only, most populated Northern city)
- North East: 0 registries
- North West: 0 registries

INTERPRETATION:
This is NOT random. Two-thirds of Nigeria's cancer registries are in the
South, with the North almost completely absent. This reflects:

1. Infrastructure investment patterns (South has more hospitals)
2. Research capacity concentration (universities in South)
3. Economic disparities (funding follows wealth)
4. Urban-rural divides (registries need stable infrastructure)

CRITICAL IMPLICATION:
We cannot say "cancer burden is lower in the North." We can only say
"cancer burden in the North is invisible in published data." The absence
of data is not the absence of disease.

EVIDENCE:
- Ibadan and Abuja registries: Jedy-Agba et al. 2012, PubMed 22621842
- Calabar, Ekiti, Enugu: NCBI Bookshelf cancer registry summaries
- All registries: population-based (not hospital-based), so ASR comparisons valid
- Study periods: 2009-2016 (recent enough to be relevant)

================================================================================

FINDING 3: SURVEILLANCE INEQUALITY IS THE REAL STORY
-----------------------------------------------------

Complete Coverage (All 6 zones):
- Diabetes: YES - Uloko et al. 2018 includes all zones
- National inference: POSSIBLE

Sparse Coverage (6 states only, 16% of Nigeria):
- Cancer: NO - only 6 registries, 5 in South
- National inference: NOT POSSIBLE

What This Means for Public Health Policy:
- We know diabetes burden is high across Nigeria, including the North
- We do NOT know cancer burden in 31 Nigerian states
- Health system strengthening in cancer surveillance is needed, especially
  in North-East, North-West (zero registries) and parts of North Central
- Resource allocation for cancer prevention/treatment cannot be evidence-based
  in most of Nigeria

This is a systems-level health equity problem.

GEOGRAPHIC PATTERNS (Same as Project 1)
=======================================

South-North Divide Appears in Both Projects:

Project 1 (Pidgin English):
- 90% speakers in South (Rivers, Bayelsa)
- 25% speakers in North (Sokoto)
- Language follows trade and urban history

Project 2 (NCDs):
- Diabetes: 9.8% in South South vs 3.0% in North West
- Cancer: All registries in South (except 1 in Abuja)
- Disease burden and surveillance both South-concentrated

Interpretation:
This isn't coincidence. The South-North divide in Nigeria reflects:
- Urbanization (South > North)
- Economic activity (oil, trade in South)
- Health system infrastructure (better resourced in South)
- Research capacity (universities, hospitals in South)

These are structural, not natural patterns. Understanding them matters for
evidence-based policy.

================================================================================

METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
====================

Why Zone Level for Diabetes, Registry Level for Cancer?

Diabetes: Uloko et al. meta-analysis reports zone-level aggregates.
Raw data not available; zone is the smallest geographic unit.

Cancer: No national registry exists. Only individual, isolated registries.
These happen to be in 6 states scattered across Nigeria.

Could we do state-level diabetes? Not from published sources. State-level
data would require accessing NDHS raw datasets and performing custom analysis.

Could we do zone-level cancer? No. Registries don't group by zone; we can't
aggregate scattered registries into meaningful zone estimates without
fabricating data.

The lesson: This project shows an even more greater problem;
Data scientists in Nigeria, struggle to make evidence based decisions due to fragmented health data.

================================================================================

LIMITATIONS
=======================================

1. TEMPORAL MISMATCH
   - Diabetes: Uloko et al. meta-analysis pools studies 2005-2018
   - Cancer: Registries are 2009-2016
   - Not from the exact same year, but close enough for geographic pattern

2. DIABETES IS ZONE LEVEL, CANCER IS STATE LEVEL
   - Cannot calculate zone-level cancer burden
   - Cannot calculate state-level diabetes burden
   - Cannot directly correlate at one level
   - This is why we overlay instead of merge

3. CANCER DATA IS SPARSE
   - Only 6 registries; cannot infer national cancer burden
   - Cannot say "cancer is lower in the North"; can only say "invisible"

4. NO INDIVIDUAL-LEVEL DATA
   - All aggregated by geography
   - Cannot link individual diabetes + cancer status

These limitations has shone light on a big issue with health data systems in Nigeria.
